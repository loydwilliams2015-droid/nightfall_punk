# nightfall!punk — Legal, provenance, attribution, and fair-use policy

**Stewardship status:** repository policy draft for review; not legal advice.

## 1. Source-code incorporation

Shipping code must have a traceable basis: project authorship, compatible license, or explicit permission. **Do not incorporate third-party code merely because a fair-use argument might exist.** When adapting licensed code, retain required notices, identify the upstream source/version, and record modifications.

Mechanism inspiration is not code provenance. Reading how Quake, GZDoom, Unreal, Tomb Raider, or another project solves a problem may inform a clean implementation; comparative study does not authorize copying protected source/assets outside their license.

## 2. Research references and plagiarism

- Attribute specific ideas, algorithms, datasets, quotations, screenshots, diagrams, and borrowed terminology when materially derived from an external source.
- Prefer paraphrase plus citation over copying.
- Quotations should be only as long as the analytical purpose requires.
- Never present another project's code, art, prose, experiments, or results as nightfall-authored.
- Preserve uncertainty: a source can be credible for one mechanism without becoming authority for unrelated claims.
- Maintain a distinction among **copied/adapted**, **independently implemented from documented behavior**, and **merely inspired/comparative** work.

## 3. Fair use

U.S. fair use is case-specific. For commentary, criticism, scholarship, research, and documentation, evaluate purpose/character, nature of the work, amount/substantiality, and market effect. There is no project "safe percentage." Prefer the minimum necessary excerpt and avoid substituting for the original work.

For distributable game/code assets, rely on licenses or permission rather than fair use whenever practicable.

## 4. AI-assisted development

Substantive AI assistance should be disclosed in the relevant ledger/PR when it materially shaped code, tests, prose, or analysis. AI-generated output is not treated as proof of originality or license compatibility. Review suspiciously source-like output before use, test all executable work, and assign human/project acceptance separately from drafting assistance.

## 5. Commercial and nonprofit goals

Software licensing, governance, and business model are separate layers. A genuine open-source license must permit commercial use. Copyleft can coexist with paid distribution, support, hosting, integration, bespoke development, training, and stewardship services. Do not add a "noncommercial" restriction and still describe the software as open source.

A dual-licensing or proprietary-exception model requires a rights-ownership/contributor agreement analysis before adoption. Do not introduce copyright assignment or a CLA casually.

## 6. Trademarks and comparative names

Third-party names identify comparison targets only. Do not imply endorsement, affiliation, or official compatibility without evidence. Original demos inspired by another game's design space must be named and presented as original nightfall!punk work unless they truly implement a licensed compatibility layer.

## 7. Release gate

Before public distribution, confirm: project license status; third-party notices; source/art/data provenance; redistribution requirements; generated assets; privacy/telemetry; trademark presentation; and the evidence level actually supported.
